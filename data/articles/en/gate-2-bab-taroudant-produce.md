## The souk the city actually eats from

**Bab Taroudant** — Gate 2 — leads into the produce section that sits at the centre of the whole market. This is the least touristy and most honest wing of [Souk Al Ahad](/en/history/from-talborjt-to-amsernat): crates of tomatoes, mint by the armful, Souss strawberries in winter, clementines in autumn, herbs, dates in dozen varieties, and the seasonal rhythm of the entire Souss plain on display.

Tourists come for carpets; Agadir comes here. That alone makes it worth twenty minutes of your time.

## What to do here

- **Buy fruit you've never seen.** Prickly pears (karmous), sugar bananas, quinces, loquats when in season. Prices are per kilo and largely fixed — this is locals' commerce, not theatre.
- **Drink the juice.** Fresh orange or pomegranate juice, pressed while you wait, for pocket change. In the heat of the day it is the best transaction in Agadir.
- **Eat m'semen.** The flaky square flatbread, cooked on griddles around the edges of the section — a few dirhams, best consumed hot standing up.

## Good to know

- The produce section is the easiest place to photograph people working normally. Ask, smile, buy something — permission flows from courtesy, and a ten-dirham bag of dates is the best photo licence in Morocco.
- Prices here are close to fixed. Bargaining belongs to [the craft gates](/en/visit/gate-3-bab-sijilmassa-pottery), not to the woman selling you mint.
