## The gate you smell first

**Bab Tombouctou** — Gate 1 of the thirteen — opens directly into the spice quarter, the most photographed and most fragrant wing of [Souk Al Ahad](/en/history/from-talborjt-to-amsernat). Traders stack their wares in cones and pyramids: turmeric, paprika, cumin, cinnamon bark, dried rose buds, ras el hanout blends of thirty spices or more. Further in, the olive sellers: vats of cracked, salted, lemon-cured and harissa-punched olives, sold by the scoop.

## What to buy here

- **Spice blends** — ras el hanout, chermoula, harissa powder. Ask what's in the blend; a good seller can name every component.
- **Olives and preserved lemons** — the base of every Moroccan tagine. Sold by weight, priced per kilo.
- **Henna and ghassoul clay** — cosmetic staples, cheap and genuine here.

## How not to get burned

- **Buy where turnover is high.** Pyramids that look ancient have been refilled a thousand times — ask for spice scooped from the sack behind the stall, not the display cone, which sits in sun and dust all day.
- **Real saffron is never cheap and never sold in loose, generous piles.** If a "saffron" price seems too good, you are looking at dyed corn silk or safflower.
- **Spices lose their punch in weeks.** Buy what you'll use in three months, not a spice-rack's worth of souvenirs.

Prices for ordinary spices are low and mostly fixed; heavy bargaining belongs to the craft sections, not here. For the ritual itself, read [Bargaining 101](/en/visit/bargaining-101).
