## The name is a memory

"Souk Al Ahad" means **the Sunday market** — the name survives from the weekly gathering in old Talborjt, before [the earthquake moved the souk to Amsernat](/en/history/from-talborjt-to-amsernat). Sixty years later the market runs six days a week — but the Sunday instinct never died. It is still the day the whole region converges.

## The crowd calendar

- **Sunday:** up to **~30,000 visitors** — families, countryside buyers, day-trippers. Peak energy, peak squeeze.
- **Saturday:** the second-busiest day; families doing the weekly shop.
- **Tuesday–Thursday:** the calm days, roughly the weekday baseline of ~10,000 — your window for unhurried [bargaining](/en/visit/bargaining-101).
- **Friday:** calm midday (prayer), lively again by late afternoon.
- **Monday:** closed — [cleaning day](/en/visit/hours-why-monday-closes).

## What each day is for

**Come Sunday if** you want the spectacle: the souk at maximum volume, produce mountains at their tallest, the fish wing shouting, whole families negotiating carpets. Photographers and people-watchers: this is your day. Go before 10:00, keep valuables zippered [crowd-pocket rules](/en/visit/souk-safety-honest-answer), and surrender to the river.

**Come Tuesday–Thursday if** you came to buy. Sellers have time, tea, and patience; your [two-hour loop](/en/visit/first-visit-two-hour-plan) breathes; the carpet ceremony at [Gate 4](/en/visit/gate-4-bab-tamdoult-carpets) runs at full ritual instead of fast-forward.

## The local's trick

Shop weekday mornings for crafts; do food on any day at opening. And if you're only in Agadir on a Sunday — go anyway. Crowded is the correct way to meet a Sunday market; it's literally what it's named after.
