## Eat the market

The souk is Agadir's pantry, and its best food is not in restaurants — it's at the gates. The loop below costs less than a hotel breakfast and is more honest than any of them.

## The eating loop

1. **Breakfast at Gate 2 — m'semen and juice.** The flaky square flatbread comes off griddles around the produce wing while it's still too hot to hold; a glass of orange or pomegranate is pressed in front of you. Together: **~15 MAD**. Standing up, like the regulars. ([Gate 2 guide](/en/visit/gate-2-bab-taroudant-produce))
2. **Snack through Gate 1 — dates and olives.** Ask for a tasting and you'll get one; buying a small mixed bag of dates (varieties have names — ask) is the correct response to the hospitality. ([Gate 1 guide](/en/visit/gate-1-bab-tombouctou-spices))
3. **Taste at Gate 9 — amlou.** Argan, almonds, honey — most argan stalls will let you taste it on bread. You will then understand why 250 g jars exist in luggage. ([Gate 9 guide](/en/visit/gate-9-bab-imouzzar-argan-soaps))
4. **Lunch at Gate 11 — the sardine grill.** Charcoal, smoke, sardines straight off the Atlantic boats: a plate with bread, salad and lemon for **30–50 MAD**, eaten where the workers eat. The queue is the review. ([Gate 11 guide](/en/visit/gate-11-bab-tiznit-fish))
5. **Tea, anywhere it's offered.** At the carpet shops it's part of [the negotiation ritual](/en/visit/bargaining-101) — a glass commits you to nothing but the twenty minutes.

## Rules of the road

- **The queue is the health certificate.** Eat where locals queue; turnover is freshness.
- **Bottled water** — the tap is fine for teeth, not for travellers' stomachs.
- **Seasonal courage:** prickly pears in summer (peeled by the seller), sugar bananas, loquats — ask what's best *today*.
- **Ramadan:** the afternoon food wing building toward iftar is the most beautiful spectacle the souk offers — [what to know](/en/visit/ramadan-at-the-souk).
