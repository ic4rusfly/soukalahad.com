## The port comes to the wall

Agadir owns one of the largest fishing ports in Africa, and its catch walks through **Bab Tiznit** — Gate 11 — every morning: sardines, mackerel, squid, sea bream, langoustine, whatever the Atlantic offered at dawn. The fish wing of [Souk Al Ahad](/en/history/from-talborjt-to-amsernat) is a working market with ice, scales and zero ceremony.

## What to know

- **The clock rules.** Serious buying happens before noon. Late afternoon is slim pickings.
- **Prices are per kilo and near-fixed** — this is commerce for locals. Sardines are famously cheap; langoustine and bream climb accordingly.
- **Look at the eyes and the smell.** Clear eyes, Atlantic brine, firm flesh. Agadir's turnover is fast enough that this is usually a formality — it's a busy port city's market.

## The lunch

Around and just inside the gate, grills cook sardines over charcoal through the middle of the day. A plate — sardines, bread, maybe a salad and a wedge of lemon — costs what a coffee costs on the promenade, and it is one of the best meals in Agadir. Stand where the workers eat; the queue is the review.

**Combine it:** the fish wing sits naturally at the end of the food loop — [produce at Gate 2](/en/visit/gate-2-bab-taroudant-produce), [butchers at Gate 10](/en/visit/gate-10-bab-oujda-butchers), fish here.
