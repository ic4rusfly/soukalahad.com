## The wing that dresses everyone

**Bab Igli** — Gate 5 — is the leather gate: walls of babouches (the pointy traditional slippers) in every colour, handbags, satchels, travel bags, belts, jackets and the leather poufs that end up in living rooms from Berlin to London. Leather is Morocco's oldest export craft, and this is its Agadir headquarters.

## The tests that matter

- **The smell test settles everything.** Real leather smells of skin and tan; a sharp chemical reek means chrome-heavy cheap tanning or synthetic coating.
- **Look at the edge.** Cut or burnished edges show fibre structure; plastic-looking, perfectly smooth edges on a "leather" bag mean bonded leather — shredded hide glued to a fabric back, the stuff that peels in a year.
- **Feel the back.** Genuine leather is suede-like or fibrous on the reverse; synthetics show weave or foam.
- **Bend it.** Real leather creases and recovers; synthetic cracks or stays dimpled.

## What things cost, honestly

A pair of simple leather babouches starts around 60–100 MAD; a well-made handbag runs a few hundred dirhams; a good full-leather jacket is a four-figure affair that is still far cheaper than its European equivalent. Opening prices will be generously above that — [bargaining is expected and welcome here](/en/visit/bargaining-101).

## Good to know

- Stitching tells the truth about construction: even, tight, reinforced at the straps.
- Sheepskin is soft and light; goat is tougher; cowhide is the heavy-duty option. Say which one you want.
- Saturday is the souk at its most crowded; weekday mornings are calm and sellers are fresher — see [the two-hour first visit plan](/en/visit/first-visit-two-hour-plan).
