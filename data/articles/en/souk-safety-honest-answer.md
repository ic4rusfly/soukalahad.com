## The honest answer

Yes. Souk Al Ahad is a working market that tens of thousands of families use every day, with a police post inside the walls and crowds from opening to close. Violent crime against visitors is rare. The real risks are boring: pickpockets in crowds, commission "guides", and taxis without meters. Manage those three and you're safer here than in most tourist districts of Europe's capitals.

## The three real risks

- **Crowd pockets.** At [peak Sunday crowds](/en/visit/sunday-vs-weekday-crowds), alleys compress and pickpockets work the squeeze. Carry the day's cash in your front pocket, cards and passport at the hotel, and nothing worth panicking about.
- **The volunteer guide.** A friendly stranger offers to show you "the real souk" and delivers you to a shop; his commission is in your price. A warm *"la, shukran"* (no, thank you) and walking on ends it — see [greetings and etiquette](/en/visit/etiquette-greetings).
- **The unmetered taxi.** Agree the price before the door closes, or insist on the meter: *"le compteur, s'il vous plaît."* A ride to the souk is roughly **15–30 MAD** — [full transport notes in the first-visit plan](/en/visit/first-visit-two-hour-plan).

## Numbers worth keeping

- **Police: 19** (city) · **Gendarmerie: 177** · **Ambulance: 150**
- The souk has its own **police post inside the walls** — ask any shopkeeper to point you to it; they will, gladly.

## For solo travellers and women

Daytime, the souk is completely normal — you will be walking among local women doing their shopping. Attention can be persistent; it is annoyance, not danger. A firm no, sunglasses-off confidence, and shopping where families shop (the food wings) keeps it pleasant. Late evening, stick to the lit main alleys around [Bab Demnat](/en/visit/gate-13-bab-demnat-south-gate).

## The honest exceptions

Carry cash — cards barely exist inside ([why](/en/visit/what-things-cost-price-list)). Watch your phone in squeezes; it's the single most stolen object on earth, and the souk is on earth. And a "free argan demonstration" that ends in a pressure sale room is a [fake-argan risk](/en/visit/argan-oil-real-or-fake), not a safety one — read the guide, skip the demo.

Go in the morning, keep your wits about you, and you'll leave with souvenirs and no stories you didn't want.
