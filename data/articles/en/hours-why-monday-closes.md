## The hours, plainly

- **Tuesday → Sunday:** roughly **9:00 to 19:00** in winter, stretching toward **21:00** in summer
- **Monday: CLOSED** — the whole souk, all 13 gates
- **Friday:** a lull around the midday prayer; food sellers stay busiest
- **Public and religious holidays:** hours shift — Eid weeks transform the place

## Why Monday?

Because a market that feeds a city needs a weekly bath. Souk Al Ahad is 11–13 hectares of produce, fish, spice dust and tens of thousands of footsteps a day — [a city organ, not a theme park](/en/visit/gate-10-bab-oujda-butchers). Monday is the traditional cleaning day: alleys washed, drainage flushed, stalls restocked, walls and reed roofs maintained. It has worked this way for decades, and it's part of why the souk stays as functional as it is.

Show up on a Monday and you'll meet the wall, the closed gates, and a handful of shops in the surrounding streets — nothing more.

## The best hours, ranked

1. **9:00–11:00 — the golden window.** Cool, calm, fresh produce stacked, sellers unhurried, [bargaining](/en/visit/bargaining-101) at its friendliest. Light for photos at [the gates](/en/visit/gate-13-bab-demnat-south-gate) is soft and low.
2. **16:00–18:00 — the golden light.** Warmer atmosphere, lanterns coming alive at [Gate 3](/en/visit/gate-3-bab-sijilmassa-pottery), still manageable crowds on weekdays.
3. **Friday midday — the quiet hour.** Prayer-time lull; respectful calm, half-empty alleys, but some stalls shutter.

## Avoid

- **Sunday midday** if you dislike crowds — [30,000 visitors](/en/visit/sunday-vs-weekday-crowds) is a river, not a stream.
- **The last hour**: shutters come down fast and grumpily; nothing good is negotiated at closing time.

**Bottom line:** Tuesday or Wednesday at 9:15, breakfast m'semen at [Gate 2](/en/visit/gate-2-bab-taroudant-produce), and the souk is yours.
