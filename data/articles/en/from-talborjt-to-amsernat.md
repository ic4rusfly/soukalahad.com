## A market that met once a week

The name tells you how old the ritual is: **Souk El Had — Souk Al Ahad — "the Sunday market"**. Before 1960, it was exactly that: a weekly market held in the **Talborjt** quarter, the old heart of Agadir. Farmers and herders from the Souss plain, artisans from the Anti-Atlas, traders with dates, argan, wool, salt and cloth — they arrived, raised tents and laid out mats, traded for a day, and the city's week was measured around it.

A weekly souk is not just shopping. In southern Morocco it was the region's calendar, its news network and its bank — the day accounts were settled, marriages arranged, and harvests priced.

## Exile to the industrial quarter

On the night of [29 February 1960](/en/history/agadir-earthquake-1960), the earthquake destroyed Talborjt along with most of the city. In the reconstruction that followed, the market was moved out of the ruins to **Amsernat**, beside the new industrial zone — according to the souk's own heritage record, on the site of the old municipal slaughterhouse.

What began as displacement became an institution. The tents turned into lots; the lots into shops; the weekly market into a permanent one, enclosed by walls six to eight metres high and pierced by **thirteen gates**. The traders kept the old name — the Sunday market — even as the souk opened six days a week. (It closes on Mondays, the traditional cleaning day.)

## What the move became

Over six decades, the exiled market grew into **Africa's largest urban market**:

- Around **6,000 shops** (estimates run from 3,000 to 6,000 — no one has ever counted them all)
- **11 to 13 hectares** behind the walls, organised into craft zones: spices, produce, carpets, leather, jewellery, pottery, electronics
- Roughly **10,000 visitors a day** — and up to **30,000** on Sundays and public holidays

The **2021 renovation** gave the souk the face it shows today: walls of rammed earth combined with concrete, roofs of coloured reeds, and great carved wooden gates named after the cities and regions of Moroccan memory — **Bab Tombouctou, Bab Taroudant, Bab Sijilmassa, Bab Tiznit** and the rest.

## The paradox worth remembering

The earthquake that destroyed Agadir's old town also created the modern souk: without the move to Amsernat, there would have been no room — and perhaps no reason — for a market of this scale. The "Sunday market" that lost its home became the commercial lung of the new Agadir, and the line of traders running through it was never broken.
