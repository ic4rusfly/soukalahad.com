## A different clock

For one month a year, the souk keeps the fast's schedule. Nothing stops — this is still the market that feeds the city — but the rhythm inverts:

- **Mornings:** calm, late openings, unhurried sellers
- **Afternoon:** the food wings build to a crescendo — the **iftar rush**, buyers loading dates, honey, chebakia, harira herbs and everything that lands on the sunset table
- **After sunset:** the souk fills again; the evening is social, shopping runs late, alleys hum
- **The last ten days and Eid prep:** the [clothing gate](/en/visit/gate-6-fashion-djellabas) and shoe sellers at full intensity — Eid is the retail event of the year

## Visiting respectfully

- **Don't eat, drink or smoke in the alleys during the day.** Nobody will arrest you; everybody will appreciate you not doing it. Water discreetly at a café if you must.
- **"Ramadan mubarak"** is the greeting of the month — you'll get a smile every time (more greetings in [the etiquette guide](/en/visit/etiquette-greetings)).
- **Cafés inside the walls may close or run dark during the day**; they explode back to life after sunset.
- **Bargain gently.** Everyone's fasting, including the man negotiating your carpet. Keep it warm, keep it short — the [rules of dignity](/en/visit/bargaining-101) matter double this month.

## Why Ramadan is secretly a great time to come

The afternoon food wing is one of the most beautiful spectacles the souk produces: pyramids of dates in a dozen varieties, trays of chebakia glowing in honey, mint by the tonne. And the post-iftar evening souk — lit, full, unhurried — is the closest you'll get to the market as Agadir itself knows it.

Check dates before planning: Ramadan moves ~11 days earlier each year, and opening hours shift with prayer times — see [hours and Monday](/en/visit/hours-why-monday-closes).
