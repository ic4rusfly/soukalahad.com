## It is not a fight, it is a duet

Bargaining at [Souk Al Ahad](/en/history/from-talborjt-to-amsernat) is a ritual with rules older than the walls. Nobody expects your first number. Nobody is insulted by the game. The seller who laughs at your offer is still playing; the transaction usually ends with both sides satisfied and often with tea. Here is how to play it well.

## Where bargaining applies — and where it doesn't

**Bargain:** carpets, leather, pottery, lanterns, jewellery, craft goods, "antiques". 
**Don't bargain:** food basics — mint, tomatoes, bread, juice — where prices per kilo are for everyone. Pushing hard on a handful of dates isn't savvy; it's just rude.

## The mechanics

1. **Ask the price.** Nod, look mildly interested. Never gasp — shock is information.
2. **Counter at 40–50% of the opening** on craft goods. On a 300 MAD opening for a leather bag, begin at 120–150.
3. **Meet in waves.** They drop to 250, you rise to 180. The middle (200–230 here) usually closes it.
4. **The walk-away is your strongest move.** Thank them, mean it, leave. A real final price often follows you three steps out the door. If it doesn't, you have your answer — and there are six thousand other shops.
5. **Bundle to break deadlock.** Two babouche pairs plus a belt at one seller changes every number. "What's the price for all three?" is the most powerful sentence in the souk.
6. **Close gracefully.** When you shake on a number, it's done. Renegotiating after the handshake burns the goodwill you'll want next visit.

## Rules of dignity (both directions)

- Never name a number you wouldn't happily pay. If your number is accepted instantly, pay it happily.
- Decide your ceiling **before** the tea arrives at the carpet shop — see [how carpets are actually priced](/en/visit/gate-4-bab-tamdoult-carpets).
- Cash, small notes. Nobody has change for a 200 MAD note on a 40 MAD deal — or they claim not to.
- Fair ≠ cheap. A price that lets the seller eat is part of the point. Souks live on relationships, and you're building one.
