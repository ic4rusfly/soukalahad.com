## The glitter gate

**Bab Oued Noun** — Gate 7 — is the silver and gemstone wing: Amazigh fibula brooches, heavy cuffs, Tuareg crosses of Agadez, coral, amber, malachite and the famous silver work associated with **Tiznit**, the silversmith city two hours south of Agadir. This is where the souk's oldest trade and its smoothest salespeople share a counter.

## Real silver, real amber

- **Silver marks.** Handmade Amazigh silver is often *not* stamped 925 — it is frequently coin silver (around 80% pure), so weight, sound and tarnish behaviour matter more than a stamp. Machine pieces aimed at tourists usually carry the 925 mark. Price should follow weight; ask what today's silver price per gram is and do arithmetic out loud.
- **The amber trap.** Much "amber" is copal, resin, or plain plastic. Saturated salt water: real amber floats, most fakes sink. A hot needle touch on an inconspicuous spot smells of pine on real amber, acrid chemicals on plastic. Vendors who sell honestly enjoy these tests; enjoy watching the ones who don't.
- **Coral and turquoise** are frequently dyed or synthetic. Natural coral is expensive and duller than you expect; electric-bright red at a low price is glass.

## How it's sold

Jewellery is a **full-ceremony [bargaining](/en/visit/bargaining-101) zone** — tea, tray, patience. The opening price on a heavy silver piece can be double the closing one. Decide your ceiling before the tray comes out, and weigh the piece yourself if a scale appears.
