## Why ten words matter

You don't need fluent anything — you need the ten words that signal respect. The souk lives in **Darija** (Moroccan Arabic) and, deeply, in **Tachelhit**, the Amazigh language of the Souss. A greeting in Tachelhit in Agadir is not a party trick; it's a door opener.

## The words

**Darija / Arabic — everywhere:**
- **Salam alaykum** — peace upon you (the greeting; the answer is *wa alaykum salam*)
- **Bshhal?** — how much?
- **Ghali bzaf** — too expensive (say it smiling)
- **Wakha** — OK / deal
- **La, shukran** — no, thank you
- **Shukran bzaf** — thank you very much
- **Afak** — please (the most underused word by visitors)
- **Bslama** — goodbye

**Tachelhit — the Souss honours it:**
- **Azul** — hello (use it and watch faces change)
- **Tanmirt** — thank you

French works almost everywhere in Agadir; prices are often quoted in it first.

## Etiquette that actually counts

- **The right hand** gives, receives and eats. Left hands carry, they don't handshake.
- **Tea is part of commerce.** Accepting a glass at the carpet shop commits you to nothing but twenty minutes of civilisation. Refusing politely is fine; accepting is better.
- **Photographs: ask first.** The universal contract: ask, smile, and buy something small — a ten-dirham bag of dates is the best photo licence in Morocco ([more](/en/visit/gate-2-bab-taroudant-produce)).
- **Friday midday:** shops thin for prayer; don't schedule your big negotiation then — [the hours guide](/en/visit/hours-why-monday-closes).
- **"Inshallah"** attached to a delivery date is not a promise; attach dates in writing for anything you ship.
- **Dress** like you're visiting a working city, not a beach — shoulders covered does 90% of the work.

Greet first, always — before the price, before the question. A souk that greets you back is a different souk.
