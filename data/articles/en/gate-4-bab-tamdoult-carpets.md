## Where the serious money changes hands

**Bab Tamdoult** — Gate 4 — opens into the carpet and textile section: stacked wool rugs from the Anti-Atlas and the Souss, flat-woven kilims (hanbel), wedding blankets embroidered with sequins, handira capes, and bolts of cloth. This is the souk's most beautiful — and most skilfully sold — department. Everything you've heard about tea, ceremony and patience as sales technique happens here.

## Handmade or machine?

- **Flip it.** A hand-knotted rug shows the pattern on the back, knot by knot; a machine-made one shows a grid or a glued backing.
- **Feel the wool.** Sheep's wool has lanolin and slight irregularity; synthetics feel plasticky and suspiciously uniform.
- **Count the irregularities.** Slight asymmetry in the pattern is the signature of a human hand — perfect symmetry at a "handmade" price is a lie.

## What rugs honestly cost

Handmade wool rugs are priced by size, age, knot density and wool quality — a small contemporary kilim and an old high-knot piece are different universes. Any firm number printed on the internet will be used against you at the negotiating table, so treat the opening price as the start of [the bargaining ritual](/en/visit/bargaining-101), expect a long session with tea, and be genuinely ready to walk away — the walking-away is the price signal.

## Good to know

- Old rugs, "vintage" rugs and newly-dressed-as-old rugs all exist. If age matters to you, buy from a seller who can tell you the rug's region and tribe without hesitation.
- Shipping a rolled rug by courier is routine; sellers do it weekly. Negotiate item and shipping separately.
