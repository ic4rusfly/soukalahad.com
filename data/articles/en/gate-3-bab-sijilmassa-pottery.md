## The heavyweight crafts gate

**Bab Sijilmassa** — Gate 3 — is the gateway to the workshops' retail front: pottery from Safi and Tamegroute, carved wood, wrought-iron lanterns, brass trays and the punched-metal lampshades that throw star patterns on every riad ceiling in Morocco. If your suitcase has a fragile centre, it will be filled behind this gate.

## What to buy here

- **Tagines** — the iconic cone-topped pot. Decorative ones are glazed, bright and unglazed inside; **cooking tagines are unglazed or partially glazed terracotta**.
- **Tamegroute green pottery** — the signature green glaze of the deep south; each piece slightly bent, which is the proof of the kiln, not a defect.
- **Lanterns and lampshades** — brass and copper, from palm-sized to person-sized.

## How to buy ceramics that survive

- **Tap it.** A clean ring means no cracks; a dull note means it has a fracture hiding under the glaze.
- **Ask if the glaze is food-safe.** Decorative glazes can contain lead — for cooking, buy unglazed terracotta or certified food-safe glaze, and say so.
- **Negotiate, then bundle.** Ceramics are craft goods: expect the [bargaining ritual](/en/visit/bargaining-101) in full. Buying three pieces changes the arithmetic dramatically.
- **Shipping.** Big lanterns and bulky ceramics are shipped home every day — ask for a packed price including carton and foam, and pay after watching it wrapped.

Nearby: [Gate 4 — carpets and textiles](/en/visit/gate-4-bab-tamdoult-carpets), the souk's most beautiful bargaining arena.
