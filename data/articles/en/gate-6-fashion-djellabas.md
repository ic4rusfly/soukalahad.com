## Where Agadir dresses up

Gate 6 has no ancient name — it is simply the fashion gate: ready-to-wear for the whole family, and the reason many locals pass through [the souk](/en/history/from-talborjt-to-amsernat) weekly. For a visitor it holds the two most photogenic garments in Morocco: the **djellaba** (the hooded full-length robe, worn by men and women alike) and the **kaftan** (the formal, embroidered occasion piece).

## How to judge a djellaba

- **Fabric first.** Winter djellabas are heavy wool; summer ones cotton or linen. Synthetic blends are cheaper, hotter, and announce themselves by feel.
- **The trim tells the truth.** The braid edging — *sfifa* — and the hood's construction separate careful work from fast fashion. Run a finger along it: even, tight, symmetrical.
- **Fit is generous by design.** The hood (*qob*) should sit structured, not floppy. Sizes are loose; try it on — sellers expect it.
- **Price ladder.** Simple summer djellabas start in the low hundreds of dirhams; good wool and hand-trimmed pieces climb steeply from there. Kaftans — often hours of embroidery — are a different universe, and priced like it.

## Good to know

- This is a mid-intensity [bargaining](/en/visit/bargaining-101) zone: expect a real negotiation on the wool, token haggling on basics.
- Quality varies stall to stall more than gate to gate — compare three sellers before you commit.
- The most useful question in the wing: *"Made where?"* — you'll hear Taroudant, Fez, or "imported", in that order of pride.
