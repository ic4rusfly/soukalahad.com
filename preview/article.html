15 Seconds That Erased a City — the 1960 Agadir Earthquake

September 28, 2026 · 4 min read

The night Agadir fell

At around 23:40 on the night of 29 February 1960 — in the middle of Ramadan — the ground under Agadir moved for roughly fifteen seconds. The magnitude was moderate, about 5.8, but two details made it catastrophic: the epicentre lay almost underneath the city, and the rupture was shallow. A moderate quake, directly beneath a sleeping city, is the worst combination in seismology.

Agadir in 1960 was a fast-growing port and beach resort of some 40,000–50,000 people. A 16th-century kasbah — Agadir Oufella — stood on the hill above the port. Below it spread the modern “new town” of boulevards and hotels, and between them the dense traditional quarters: Talborjt, Founty and Yachech.

What fifteen seconds did

More than 90% of the buildings in the central districts collapsed. The kasbah was destroyed. Talborjt — the old heart of town — was hit hardest. The hospital, the new luxury Hôtel Saada and the National Militia headquarters all came down. Fire spread through the ruins in the hours that followed.

  • An estimated 12,000–15,000 dead — roughly a third of the city’s population
  • Around 25,000 injured
  • Between 35,000 and 50,000 homeless

The hill that remembers

On the Oufella hill today, the outer wall of the old kasbah has been restored. But the hill itself was deliberately left as a memorial — there are still unrecovered dead beneath it. Stand there at sunset and you are standing on the grave of the old Agadir.

Stand on the Oufella hill at sunset, look south across the white city — then walk down into Souk Al Ahad. That single walk is the whole story of Agadir.
